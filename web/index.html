Airwallex Agentic Banking Hackathon · Kit 02, Intent-Bound Purchase Agent

Your agent posts what it wants to buy. Vendors compete. The winning bid becomes the card.

Outbid is a purchase-intent auction for company SaaS buying. The approved bid becomes an Airwallex virtual card that can only pay that vendor, that amount, in that currency, and the vendor's acquisition fee comes back to the buyer when the charge clears.

Open the buyer console See a vendor's console npm run demo -- --wait
The problem

Companies buy software at list price. Vendors spend a fortune to find those same companies.

When agents do the buying, that's backwards. The agent already knows exactly what it needs: the category, the seats, the budget, the term. Let vendors compete for it, and turn their acquisition budget into the buyer's discount.

01

Post the intent

The company's agent publishes an anonymous purchase intent. No company name, no balance. Vendors see the category, the seats, the budget and the term.

02

Vendors bid

Vendor agents answer against the list price, each in its own currency and billing cycle. Outbid prices every bid with Airwallex FX and ranks total cost over the term.

03

The cheapest doesn't automatically win

The agent reads the company's Airwallex balance and upcoming obligations. If the annual prepay would push cash under the reserve floor in week seven, the best monthly bid wins and a reconsider date is recorded. The rules are code; the model only explains them.

04

Approval is bound to the terms

The human approves one thing: this vendor, this amount, this currency, this cycle. Nothing broader. The card's controls are derived from that approval alone, so the agent cannot widen them.

05

The winning bid becomes the card

An Airwallex virtual card with a per-transaction limit at the approved amount, a currency allowlist and the vendor's merchant category, funded from the company's Global Account.

06

Vendors pay only for closed deals

The winning vendor prepays an acquisition fee into an escrow on Metal. When the first charge clears, the escrow splits it: most goes back to the buyer through Airwallex Payouts, the rest is Outbid's fee.

Who sees what

Two consoles. The buyer never shows its hand; the vendor never guesses.

The buyer console is where the company's agent starts: it posts the intent, watches the bids and gives approvals. Each vendor has its own vendor console, where its agent sees the intent, its own bid and, when it wins, the card and the fee. Both fill in live from the same run.

The decision keeps changing as the facts do

Balances, deadlines and prices move. So does the pick.

A price hike is declined, then negotiated

At renewal the vendor charges 22% more. The card declines it with LIMIT_EXCEEDED, the same policy that set the limit. Outbid reopens the auction and goes back to the incumbent with the best new price: match, or the company switches. Any change of terms means a new card, and the old one is frozen.

Cash recovers, so the annual bid comes back

A customer payment lands in the Global Account. The agent re-runs the same decision on the new balance. The annual prepay now stays above the reserve, so it asks the human to approve the switch, issues a new card, and freezes the old one, which then declines.

Why a card, not just a bid

A bid is a promise. The card is the enforcement.

The vendor's own billing system decides what actually gets charged, and an agent holding the company card is a blank check. One virtual card per winning bid closes three gaps at once, and almost every SaaS vendor already accepts cards, so it needs zero vendor integration.

The vendor can't overcharge

The terms are enforced by the payment network at authorization, before money moves.

The agent can't overspend

Its authority is scoped to exactly what the human approved. No prompt injection or hallucination can widen it.

Nobody can fake a sale

Every settled charge on that card is the proof of sale, reported by the network rather than by either party. That is what makes "vendors pay only for closed deals" enforceable.

Run it

No build, no dependencies. Node 20 and two sandboxes.

Get the code

git clone https://github.com/soarewee/outbid
cd outbid

Create an Airwallex sandbox account

At sandbox.airwallex.com, register the business in the United States and name it Sandbox Business so it skips review; card issuing is rejected for businesses in unsupported regions. Then create a Client ID and API key under Account → Developer → API keys.

Get a Metal testnet endpoint and a funded test key

An RPC endpoint from the Metal testnet console, and a disposable key (cast wallet new) with USDZ from the faucet. The escrow contract is already deployed; the key only funds the vendors' prepaid fees.

Configure and run

cp .env.example .env     # fill in the Airwallex and Metal keys; never commit it
npm run demo -- --wait   # then open http://localhost:4780/buyer and post the intent

The buyer console asks what to buy and the story plays from there. Open /vendor beside it to watch the vendors' side. Approvals can be given on the page or in the terminal.
