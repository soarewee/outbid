Your agent posts what it wants to buy. Vendors compete. The winning bid becomes the card.

A purchase-intent auction for the software and compute a company buys. Vendors' agents bid against each other for it, and the winning bid becomes an Airwallex virtual card whose limit is that bid, in that currency, at that kind of vendor. The vendor can't charge a cent more than it bid.

How it works

One request, one intent, one card.

AI agents are starting to buy for companies, but buying is still built for people: websites, quote requests, sales calls. An agent needs one thing, the best offer. So the agent posts what it needs, and vendors' agents compete to win it.

The approval is issued as the card

The human approves one thing: this vendor, this amount, this currency. That approval is issued as a card whose limit is the winning bid, so the network, not a policy, decides every charge.

Why a card

The approval is the card.

You approve one thing: this vendor, this amount, this currency, this kind of merchant. Outbid issues that approval as an Airwallex virtual card whose controls are exactly those terms. No policy for anyone to follow, no prompt for an agent to obey: the network checks every charge against the approval at authorization, before money moves.

The vendor can't overcharge. A vendor that bid low to win can't make it back later: a setup fee on top, or a price hike at renewal, is declined with the very limit that was approved. The agent can't overspend. It never holds the company card, only one made for this deal. The sale is on record. Every settled charge is reported by the network, and that is what Outbid reports to release the vendor's bond.

It doesn't have to be a card, but a card is convenient: almost every cloud and software vendor already takes one, so there is nothing for the vendor to integrate, and the safeguards (limits, declines, a freeze) are already built in. The card's controls are the amount, the currency and the merchant category; a vendor that is a known merchant brand at Airwallex can be bound by brand as well.

When cash decides

A cheaper prepaid year waits for the cash

Months later, a third vendor offers the term prepaid: the cheapest total yet, even after the exit fee the current vendor's bid set, which counts against every other offer. The agent reads the company's Airwallex balance and what is already due, and projects cash: paying it all up front would push cash under the reserve floor in week seven, so it waits.

When a customer payment lands, the agent re-runs the same rules on the new balance. The prepay fits now, and the switch comes with a fresh approval and a fresh card; the old card takes the exit fee as its last charge and is frozen.

What is onchain

One thing: the vendors' bid bonds

A vendor posts a bond into an escrow on Metal before it bids. Lose, and it comes straight back. Win, and it becomes Outbid's fee once the first charge clears on the card; no charge by the deadline, and the vendor takes it back itself. It is money that belongs to nobody yet, from vendors Outbid has never met, so Outbid shouldn't be the one holding it. The company's money never leaves Airwallex.

The contract can't see the card network, so the cleared charge is Outbid's report; what the contract guarantees is that a bond can only go back to its vendor or to the fee account, and which charge released it is public.

At renewal, a price hike is declined by the same limit that was approved. That decline tells the agent to reopen the auction and go back to the incumbent: match the best new price, or the company switches.

Who sees what

Two consoles. The buyer never shows its hand; the vendor never guesses.

The buyer console is where the company's agent starts: it posts the intent, watches the bids and gives approvals. Each vendor has its own vendor console, where its agent sees the intent, its own bids and, when it wins, the card and its bond. Both fill in live from the same run.

Built on

Every step runs against the Airwallex sandbox and the Metal testnet.

The agent runs on your machine. Claude, over the Messages API, does the words: it works out the intent from your request, explains each decision, and bids for each vendor inside limits that are code. Airwallex does the money: balances for the budget and the cash check, FX for cross-currency bids, Issuing for the card controls, card transactions as proof of sale, Global Accounts for funding and Payouts for the cashback. Metal holds the one thing Airwallex doesn't: the vendors' bid bonds. Each run saves its request log.

Run it

Three steps to a running auction

Node 20, no build, no dependencies. You need an Airwallex sandbox account and a Metal testnet key; both are free.

1

Get the code

Clone the repository and copy the example environment file. Nothing to install.

$ git clone https://github.com/soarewee/outbid
$ cd outbid
$ cp .env.example .env
2

Add the two sandboxes

At sandbox.airwallex.com, register the business in the United States and name it Sandbox Business so it skips review. Create a Client ID and API key under Account → Developer.

For Metal, an RPC endpoint from the testnet console and a disposable key (cast wallet new) with USDZ from the faucet. Put all of it in .env, and never commit it.

3

Run, then tell the agent what you need

Start the demo and open the buyer console. It asks what the company needs; add ANTHROPIC_API_KEY to .env and Claude writes the intent from your words, or fill in the four fields yourself. Open the vendor console beside it to watch the other side.

$ npm run demo -- --wait
→ http://localhost:4780/buyer
→ http://localhost:4780/vendor
